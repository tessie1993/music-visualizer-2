# Visual Uniqueness Spec — cymatics and hyperspace substyles

Differentiation recipes so every substyle is recognizable in three seconds. Cymatics recipes
are grounded in real acoustic phenomena; hyperspace recipes (appended below when research
completes) in the mathematics of visual pattern and visionary-art aesthetics.

## Cymatics — context

Design research for MusicViz's 11 cymatics substyles (VisualStyleCatalog.kt cymatics list; branches in cymatics_field_frag.glsl). Root cause of sameness: every branch renders the SAME base composite — nodal filigree + halo + caustic sheen + iridescent body (lines 199-244) — and the uStyle branches only add a small domain warp (styleCoordinates, lines 69-94) and a faint overlay (lines 249-287). Ten styles are therefore the Original with seasoning. The fix is per-style ownership of the transfer function from field height h to pixels: each branch must SUPPRESS or REPLACE parts of the base stack (there is precedent — Chladni Sand already does `color *= 0.46`), own a distinct motion signature (jump / drift / flip / oscillate / decay), and a distinct material palette (matte sand, opaque skin, ink on paper, glassy water, black ferrofluid). Cheapest structural move: compute the base layers as now, but let each branch set four mask floats (filigreeMask, haloMask, causticMask, fillMask) before compositing, then add its own signature layer — controls keep their meaning, no code fork. Two small plumbing additions recommended (both optional, no new render targets): (1) a float uniform uFigureSeed, incremented CPU-side in CymaticsScene.draw() when beatPulse crosses ~0.6, hashed in-shader to make discrete figures JUMP on beat (Chladni, Shell symmetry, Faraday defects); (2) an optional `modeLimit: Int?` bias on CymaticsStyle so resonance styles (Chladni, Drumhead) render 2-3 discrete modes instead of an 8-mode mush — it feeds the existing `plate.snapshot(limit, ...)` call. All recipes stay one fullscreen fragment pass; the costliest adds at most 2-3 extra field() evaluations or a few plane-wave cosines, within the 50fps supersampled budget. Recipes below are original designs; phenomenon references are for look/physics only (repo licence rule: Shadertoy default licence is CC BY-NC-SA — techniques may inform, code must not be copied).

## Cymatics references

### Reference: Chladni plate figures — discrete resonances, sand on nodal lines

Geometry: sand collects ONLY where the plate does not move — the nodal lines — leaving bare plate everywhere else; figures are discrete, high-contrast, and specific to one (n,m) resonance at a time. Motion signature: essentially static while a resonance is held; at a frequency sweep the figure JUMPS between discrete patterns, and during the transition the sand visibly dances/boils before re-settling on the new nodal set. Frequency response: higher frequency = finer, more numerous nodal lines (Chladni's law, f ~ (m+2n)^2 for plates — the app's sqrt pitch-to-order law in CymaticsMath matches). Amplitude response: louder drive bounces grains harder and cleans the nodal lines sharper; it does not change the figure. Visual material: matte metal plate, granular monochrome sand — no glow, no iridescence, no rotation.

*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Chladni (Chladni figures section); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chladni%27s_law; Harvard Natural Sciences Lecture Demonstrations 'Chladni Plates'*

### Reference: Faraday waves — subharmonic tiling lattices

Geometry: a vertically vibrated fluid surface breaks into a spatially periodic lattice covering the whole surface uniformly — stripes, square, or hexagonal tilings; no centre, no figure. Motion signature: the lattice oscillates at HALF the driving frequency (subharmonic — cells rhythmically flip peak/trough), drifts slowly, and develops defects/dislocations where domains meet. Frequency response: wavelength set by the dispersion relation — higher drive frequency = smaller cells. Amplitude response: the defining behaviour — below threshold the surface is flat; just above, stripes; higher drive selects squares then hexagons and eventually disorder. Visual material: liquid — specular cell peaks, caustic borders between cells.

*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faraday_wave; Douady 1990, 'Experimental study of the Faraday instability', J. Fluid Mech. 221*

### Reference: water cymatics / CymaScope imagery — symmetric water mandalas

Geometry: a small water-filled dish driven by pure tones and lit from below produces highly symmetric mandala imagery — concentric petal rings whose azimuthal symmetry order (petal count) is set by the tone, with flower-of-life-like lace where wave copies interfere, bounded by a bright meniscus rim. Motion signature: stately — the figure holds and shimmers while a tone is held; changing the tone reconfigures the whole mandala to a new symmetry. Frequency response: higher pitch = more petals/rings. Amplitude response: deeper relief and brighter caustic sparkle. Visual material: glassy, refractive, iridescent — the one aesthetic in the family that justifies the full caustic+iridescence stack.

*Source: https://cymascope.com (John Stuart Reid); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cymatics*

### Reference: Kundt's tube — 1D dust striations

Geometry: strictly one-dimensional — cork dust inside a horizontal glass tube gathers into regularly spaced heaps at the standing wave's displacement nodes (spacing = half wavelength), with fine transverse ripple striations in the dust near antinodes. Motion signature: dust is lofted and swirls when the drive changes or intensifies, then settles under gravity into the band pattern; band positions snap when the resonant mode changes. Frequency response: higher pitch = closer band spacing (the original experiment measured sound speed from this spacing). Amplitude response: louder = taller, more agitated heaps and stronger kick-up. Visual material: pale granular dust, glass tube highlights, dark lab background.

*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kundt%27s_tube*

### Reference: harmonograph — decaying Lissajous pen traces

Geometry: pure LINE ART — a pen driven by two (or more) damped pendulums traces x = A e^(-dt) sin(at+p1), y = B e^(-dt) sin(bt+p2): nested, slowly precessing rosettes and woven ellipses whose stroke spacing tightens toward the centre as amplitude decays. Near-rational frequency ratios give closed rosettes; detuning gives slow precession of the whole figure. Motion signature: in a finished trace, none — the record of decay IS the picture; live, the figure spirals inward. Frequency response: the ratio a:b sets the figure family (2:3 gives different rosettes than 1:2). Amplitude response: initial amplitude sets outer envelope size; damping sets stroke density gradient. Visual material: constant-width ink on paper — monochrome, matte, no fill, no glow.

*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonograph; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lissajous_curve*

### Reference: circular membrane (Bessel) drum modes

Geometry: displacement J_m(k_mn r)·cos(m·theta) inside a hard circular rim — nodal DIAMETERS (m of them) and nodal CIRCLES (n of them) partition the head into pie/annulus lobes; adjacent lobes always move in OPPOSITE phase, so the natural reading is sign-alternating two-tone regions, not lines. Motion signature: standing oscillation — lobes flip up/down through flat; a strike launches a radially decaying transient from the impact point. Frequency response: overtone series is inharmonic (zeros of J_m); higher pitch = more diameters/circles. Amplitude response: deeper doming of the lobes. Visual material: opaque taut skin (mylar/hide) with soft top light — matte, no transparency, hard rim boundary. The app's besselApprox/McMahon machinery already matches the maths; the differentiation is entirely in shading sign instead of |h|.

*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vibrations_of_a_circular_membrane; Dan Russell's acoustics animations, https://www.acs.psu.edu/drussell/Demos/MembraneCircle/Circle.html*

### Reference: acoustic levitation — droplets pinned at pressure nodes

Geometry: in a standing field between transducer and reflector, droplets/beads sit trapped slightly below the PRESSURE NODES, evenly spaced at half-wavelength intervals along the axis — a sparse vertical ladder of discrete bright points in an otherwise invisible field. Motion signature: droplets hold station with small lateral wobble; when the drive changes they oscillate about the trap before re-pinning; overdriving flattens droplets into discs and ejects them. Frequency response: higher frequency = tighter ladder spacing (lambda/2). Amplitude response: stiffer traps, stronger wobble transients on changes. Visual material: tiny specular liquid beads against a dark lab background — the field itself only visible via schlieren-style faint banding.

*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustic_levitation; Marzo et al. 2015, 'Holographic acoustic elements for manipulation of levitated objects', Nature Communications 6:8661*

### Reference: water-surface caustics — fold-caustic light webs

Geometry: refracted light through a rippling surface concentrates on the floor into thin BRIGHT connected filaments enclosing darker polygonal cells — mathematically fold caustics, where the refraction map's Jacobian vanishes, so intensity is near-singular on the filament (much sharper than any Gaussian glow). Filaments carry chromatic fringes because focal depth is wavelength-dependent. Motion signature: continuous wobble — cells stretch, merge and split; nothing is ever still. Frequency response: shorter surface ripples = finer web. Amplitude response: stronger ripples = sharper, brighter, more contorted filaments; near-flat water = broad soft patches. Visual material: light on deep water blue — the picture is the LIGHT, not the surface.

*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caustic_(optics); Evan Wallace, 'WebGL Water' technique write-up, https://madebyevan.com/webgl-water/; Shadertoy caustic studies (TECHNIQUE reference only — Shadertoy default licence CC BY-NC-SA, no code copying per repo licence rule)*

### Reference: Rosensweig (normal-field) instability — ferrofluid spike lattices

Geometry: above a critical magnetic field strength, a ferrofluid surface erupts into a HEXAGONALLY packed lattice of sharp conical peaks; below threshold it is a perfectly smooth dark mirror. Spacing set by the capillary length, remarkably regular. Motion signature: abrupt — peaks appear/disappear as the field crosses threshold and sharpen as it rises; new rings of peaks pop in from the region of strongest field; otherwise the lattice is eerily still. Frequency/drive response: for an audio mapping, drive amplitude is the natural analogue of field strength — the threshold onset is the signature to reproduce. Visual material: glossy BLACK oil-like fluid with hard white specular glints and faint oil-sheen rim colours — the least colourful, most metallic look in the family.

*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosensweig_instability; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrofluid; Cowley & Rosensweig 1967, J. Fluid Mech. 30*

### Reference: standing waves in chambers — 3D nodal planes / room modes

Geometry: a resonant enclosure supports standing waves whose nodal surfaces are PLANES (axial modes) spaced at half wavelengths along the chamber — the natural visualization is a corridor of parallel translucent sheets receding in depth, the only inherently 3D phenomenon in the set. Motion signature: sheets are stationary in space while pressure between them oscillates; a transient (a beat) launches a travelling wavefront down the chamber that reflects and settles into the standing pattern. Frequency response: higher pitch = more, closer sheets; mode changes re-space the whole stack. Amplitude response: brighter sheets, stronger travelling fronts. Visual signature to borrow: haze/fog for depth, a vanishing point, and pulses that traverse it — none of which any other substyle should have.

*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_wave; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Room_modes; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustic_resonance*
