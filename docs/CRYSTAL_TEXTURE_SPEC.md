# Crystal Texture Spec — per-theme mineral recipes

Implementation spec for the richer, more literal crystal textures the owner asked for
(reference photos supplied for Lapis, Sugilite, Rose Quartz, Amethyst; researched references
for Malachite, Clear Quartz, Kyanite, Onyx). All recipes are parametric on the theme's
primary/secondary/tertiary colors, deterministic, and bounded for 60fps Canvas drawing.

## Context

Design research for MusicViz's 8 crystal themes. Current state: drawMineralTexture in /home/user/music-visualizer-2/musicviz-project/musicviz/app/src/main/java/dev/musicviz/ui/Crystal.kt (lines 126-306) draws sparse marks at 3-13% alpha; the backdrop (CrystalBackground, lines 611-750) adds two radial washes, optional animated shards (Clear Quartz/Amethyst/Kyanite) and twinkle sparkles (Clear Quartz/Onyx/Generic); panels reuse the same function with panel=true at ~0.55 density. Theme anchors (AppTheme.kt lines 85-92) supply primary/secondary; cs.tertiary = lerp(primary, secondary, 0.5) is also derivable. Research for the 4 unphotographed stones (web snippets; Pinterest ideas hubs block direct fetch so their content is inferred from search snippets and mineral-dealer photo captions): MALACHITE = concentric wobbled ring systems ('bullseye' eyes) from botryoidal growth, thick dark-green bands alternating with thin bright bands, contour-map merging between eyes, silky chatoyant sheen, no straight bands. CLEAR QUARTZ = straight-sided tapering prisms with pyramidal terminations, milky fog at the base clearing toward tips, phantom ghost terminations, iridescent internal fractures, edge-lit glassy outlines, sparse inclusion sparkles. KYANITE = long flat blades all leaning one diagonal, fine lengthwise striations that catch raking light, feather zoning (light/dark cross-bands), deep sapphire centers fading to pale edges, pearly lit edge, white quartz matrix wedges. ONYX = straight, smooth, PARALLEL bands (explicitly straighter than agate), stark light-line/dark-gap rhythm; on the 'gold flecks?' question: metallic gold flecks are a gold-sheen-obsidian trait and gold/amber veining is architectural onyx marble — recommend keeping Onyx cool (secondary is silver-blue 0xFFA7B7D1) because gold is Lapis's signature pyrite channel; flecks should be parametric secondary-silver, never hardcoded gold. All 8 recipes below are parametric (colors expressed as lerp of primary/secondary/tertiary toward Black/White), deterministic (golden-ratio fract01 hashing or Random(fixedSeed) in remember, as the code already does), bounded (each recipe lists element budgets, 30-120 draw ops), and split into a heavy static layer (cache once per size+theme via drawWithCache path lists or a one-time ImageBitmap) plus a tiny animated glint layer (≤8-42 alpha-only tweens) so 60fps never redraws the texture. Distinguishability matrix enforced by per-recipe 'do not draw' rules: Lapis=rounded cobbles in gold seams, Malachite=closed concentric rings, Clear Quartz=pointed transparent prisms, Rose Quartz=hairline crack net in clouds, Sugilite=few large angular plates with pink fault veins, Amethyst=many small polygonal facets, Kyanite=uniform-lean blades with striations, Onyx=straight silver band lines.

## Recipes

### LAPIS recipe — blue cobbles grouted with pyrite gold

LAYERS (draw order): (1) GOLD UNDERLAY — full-rect diagonal linearGradient lerp(secondary,Black,0.35) -> secondary -> lerp(secondary,White,0.2); backdrop alpha 0.20-0.30, panel 0.08-0.12. This layer IS the pyrite grout: it shows only where nodules leave gaps, so seams come free without computing Voronoi edges. (2) NODULES — 14-22 rounded blob polygons (panel: 6-9). Placement: 4x6 grid of cell centers jittered by fract01-hash up to ±35% of cell size; blob = closed path of 6 cubic arcs, per-vertex radius r*(0.78+0.4*hash), r = 0.45-0.75 of cell — leaves 4-10% gaps between neighbors. Fill: radialGradient offset toward top-left light: 0f lerp(primary,White,0.25) [azure patch] -> 0.55f primary -> 1f lerp(primary,Black,0.45). Fill alpha HIGH: 0.55-0.80 of incoming alpha on backdrop (near-opaque cobbles are what makes the gold read as seams), 0.18-0.30 on panel. Every 3rd nodule uses cs.tertiary base for the teal cobbles in the photo. (3) SEAM RESTROKE — stroke each blob outline in secondary, width d*0.004-0.008, alpha 0.25-0.40 (panel 0.10), so grout glints even where blobs touch. (4) PYRITE GLITTER — 20-30 dots 0.5-2dp (panel 8-10) in lerp(secondary,White,0.4), alpha 0.30-0.50, placed AT blob path vertices + small jitter so flecks cluster along cracks — clustering on seams reads as pyrite-in-fractures, scattered flecks read as stars. (5) Optional inner azure: one small lightened-primary radial blob per 3rd nodule, alpha 0.10-0.18. DO NOT DRAW: the current white calcite swirls (replaced by the gold network); uniform scattered flecks away from seams; concentric rings (Malachite's); angular straight-edged plates (Sugilite's) — cobbles must be rounded. BUDGET: ≤22 blobs x (6-segment fill + stroke) + 30 dots ≈ 75 ops, fully static/cacheable.

*Source: User lapis reference photo; current impl Crystal.kt lines 139-157*

### MALACHITE recipe — bullseye ring systems with contour merging

LAYERS: (1) RIBBON WASH — 3-4 broad full-width curved diagonal strips (2 cubics each) alternating lerp(primary,Black,0.55) and lerp(primary,Black,0.25) fills, backdrop alpha 0.10-0.16, panel 0.04-0.06: deep-green depth base. (2) EYES — 2 on-screen centers + 1 straddling an edge (e.g. 0.68w/0.38h, 0.18w/0.72h, 1.02w/0.06h; hash-jitter ±5%). Each eye: 9-14 concentric CLOSED rings (panel: one eye, 5-7 rings), stroke-only. Ring geometry: 8-control-point closed cubic, radius_i = base*(1.22^i)*(1 + 0.05*sin(3a+phase_i) + 0.03*sin(5a)) — wobbled, never a perfect circle/oval (fixes the current too-regular drawOval look, lines 158-170). (3) RING COLORING — alternate: thick dark band lerp(primary,Black,0.5) width d*0.010-0.016, then thin bright band lerp(primary,White,0.30) width d*0.004-0.006; every 4th bright ring uses secondary (mint) instead. UNEQUAL widths (fat dark / hairline bright) is malachite's signature rhythm. Backdrop stroke alpha 0.18-0.35, panel 0.07-0.12. (4) MERGE CONTOURS — 4-6 open S-curve strokes (single cubic each) tangent to two eyes' outer rings, same dark color, alpha 0.12-0.20: fields read as one contour map, matching how adjacent botryoidal domes share bands. (5) CHATOYANT SILK — one diagonal white linearGradient streak across the canvas, alpha 0.05-0.08 backdrop only (skip on panel): the fibrous sheen. DO NOT DRAW: perfect circles/ellipses; straight parallel bands (Onyx's); any gold or warm hue; dots/flecks (Lapis's). BUDGET: ≤42 ring strokes + 6 contours + 4 ribbons + 1 streak ≈ 55 ops, fully static/cacheable.

*Source: FossilEra/Deep Earth Treasures/Geology Superstore slab descriptions; Reza Gem Collection + GeologyIn chatoyancy notes; Pinterest malachite-pattern hub (inferred from snippets)*

### CLEAR QUARTZ recipe — edge-lit prisms, fog, phantoms, iridescent fracture

LAYERS: (1) MILKY FOG — 2-3 big white radialGradients (radius 0.4-0.6d) anchored at bottom corners, alpha 0.05-0.10: the cloudy base every cluster grows from. (2) PRISMS — 4-6 (panel 2-3) straight-sided shafts rising from bottom/edges: 5-point path = two long side edges tapering 10-15%, topped by two termination facets meeting at an off-center tip; tilts within ±25° of vertical (varied, unlike Kyanite's uniform lean), widths 0.06-0.14w, heights 0.5-0.9h. Fill: linearGradient ALONG the shaft: base lerp(primary,White,0.1) alpha 0.10-0.12 fading to alpha 0.02 at the tip — fog clears toward terminations, exactly how real quartz reads. Alternate primary/secondary per prism (both are icy near-whites in this theme). (3) EDGE LIGHT — stroke every prism outline white alpha 0.15-0.25 width ~1dp; ONE side edge + one termination facet per prism gets a 2dp brighter stroke (shared top-left light). Quartz is drawn by its edges, not its body. (4) PHANTOMS — in 2 prisms, re-stroke the termination triangle at 60-70% scale inset toward the base, white alpha 0.08: ghost of an earlier growth stage. (5) IRIDESCENT FRACTURE — 1-2 short 3-segment polylines inside a prism stroked with a linearGradient primary -> white -> secondary along the line, alpha 0.12-0.18: theme-tinted rainbow, NOT full-spectrum hues. (6) INCLUSION SPARKLES — keep the existing animated star layer (this is the one theme where twinkles are literal inclusions) but bias ~70% of positions inside prism bounds. DO NOT DRAW: body fills above alpha 0.12 (saturated fill collapses it into generic blue crystal); polygonal mosaic (Amethyst's); uniform-direction blades (Kyanite's). BUDGET: 6 prisms x (fill+2 strokes) + 2 phantoms + 2 fractures + 3 fog blobs ≈ 30 static ops + existing 42 animated stars; prisms cache, only star alphas animate.

*Source: iStock/Dreamstime cluster descriptions; Gemmological Assoc. of Australia inclusion compilation; Paxton Gate/Mooon Crystals; Pinterest clear-quartz-aesthetic hub (inferred)*

### ROSE QUARTZ recipe — cloud body crossed by hairline crack net

LAYERS: (1) CLOUD BODY — 8-12 (panel 5-7) large soft radialGradient blobs, fract01-hashed positions, radii 0.10-0.22d, alternating white-core (0f white a0.10 -> 0.65f primary a0.05 -> transparent) and primary-core variants; overlaps create milky depth. Keeps the current blob approach but denser/larger. (2) MILKY PATCHES — 2-3 smaller, denser white blobs alpha 0.12-0.16: the opaque quartz clouds in the photo. (3) HAIRLINE CRACK NET (the signature layer) — 14-20 (panel 6-8) polylines of 2-4 straight segments with 5-15° kinks at joints, lengths 0.2-0.6d, directions fully random so they crisscross at many angles; stroke white, width 0.7-1.2px (HAIRLINE — never wider), alpha 0.10-0.20. At 4-6 hashed crossings add a 2-3px brighter white dot (junction glint). (4) ACCENT VEINS — 1-2 longer single-cubic strokes in secondary (the theme's peach 0xFFFFCBA8 = the photo's beige-gold veins, kept parametric), width d*0.0025, alpha 0.15-0.22. (5) INNER GLOW — one very soft white radial near 0.3w/0.25h alpha ~0.04, backdrop only. Panel scales alphas x0.45. DO NOT DRAW: any DARK mark — every rose quartz mark is lighter than the body; cellular nodules (Lapis); rings (Malachite); wide fracture veins — thick colored veins would turn this into Sugilite; crack thickness is the boundary between the two themes. BUDGET: ~15 gradient blobs + 20 polylines + 2 veins + 6 dots ≈ 45 ops, fully static/cacheable.

*Source: User rose quartz reference photo; current impl Crystal.kt lines 198-224*

### SUGILITE recipe — angular plates split by pink-lit diagonal faults

LAYERS: (1) FAULT PARTITION — 2-3 master polylines crossing the whole canvas at 55-70° diagonals, 3-4 segments each with 10-20° kinks, plus 1-2 shorter branch faults; endpoints/kinks fract01-hashed. These implicitly split the canvas into 4-7 plates. (2) PLATE FILLS — precompute one 4-6-vertex polygon per plate (vertices = fault kink points + canvas corners); fill each with a linearGradient between lerp(primary,Black,0.35) and lerp(primary,White,0.15), gradient direction rotated ±30° differently per plate, alpha 0.10-0.16 (panel 0.05-0.07). The value JUMP across a fault line is what reads as angular broken stone. (3) FRACTURE VEINS — restroke each fault twice: wide soft underglow in secondary (pink) width d*0.010 alpha 0.10, then a bright core line lerp(secondary,White,0.4) width d*0.0025 alpha 0.30-0.40 (panel 0.12-0.16). Rose-pink light living in the cracks is the Sugilite signature from the photo. (4) LILAC MARBLING — 2-3 wispy open cubic strokes inside plates, lerp(primary,White,0.45), width d*0.003, alpha 0.08-0.12. (5) QUARTZ CLOUD — 1-2 white radial blobs alpha 0.06-0.10 near a fault junction. (6) SPECKS — keep current dark ovals (lines 226-235) but cut to 8-12 at alpha ~0.06 (manganese inclusions). Panel: 1 fault + 2 plates + core vein only. DO NOT DRAW: gold or white-only veins (veins must read pink=secondary); rounded cobbles (Lapis); small numerous cells — plates stay LARGE (4-7 total) vs Amethyst's 24-40 facets; that count gap is the distinguishability contract. BUDGET: 7 plate fills + 3 faults x 2 strokes + 3 wisps + 2 clouds + 12 specks ≈ 30 ops, fully static/cacheable.

*Source: User sugilite reference photo; current impl Crystal.kt lines 225-244*

### AMETHYST recipe — face-on druse: polygonal facet mosaic with glints

Replace the current upward 'mountain range' triangles with the photo's face-on mosaic. LAYERS: (1) FACET MOSAIC — lay a 5x8 lattice (spacing ~0.16d), jitter each point by fract01-hash up to ±35% of spacing, connect into 24-40 triangle/quad cells covering the canvas (precompute the polygon list once; a fixed triangulation pattern over the jittered lattice is enough — no true Delaunay needed). Panel: coarser 3x4 lattice = 8-12 cells. (2) PER-FACET FILL — radialGradient centered at the cell centroid offset ~25% toward a shared top-left light: 0f lerp(primary,Black,0.5) alpha 0.16 -> 0.7f primary alpha 0.10 -> edge lerp(primary,White,0.35) alpha 0.06 — dark violet core to pale lavender rim, per the photo (note: dark CENTER, pale EDGE). Every 4th-5th cell swaps primary->secondary (pinker violet zoning); 1-2 hashed cells go near-white (milky base rock showing through). (3) JOINT LINES — stroke every cell polygon lerp(primary,White,0.6), width max(1px, d*0.0012), alpha 0.15-0.22: the thin pale joints between terminations. (4) SPECULAR GLINTS — 4-8 cells get a short bright white streak along one edge near a vertex plus a 1-2dp white dot, alpha 0.30-0.50. These are the ONLY animated elements: modulate their alpha with the existing infinite-transition phase (Amethyst already qualifies for showShards animation; repoint that budget at glints). Panel: no animation, alphas x0.45. DO NOT DRAW: crystal-point silhouettes rising from the bottom (that overlaps Clear Quartz prisms); pink/colored joints (Sugilite's); fog/cloud blobs (Rose/Clear own those); fewer-than-20 big cells (drifts into Sugilite plates). BUDGET: ≤40 cells x (fill + stroke) + 8 glints ≈ 90 ops; mosaic static/cacheable, only ≤8 glint alphas animate per frame.

*Source: User amethyst reference photo; current impl Crystal.kt lines 245-268*

### KYANITE recipe — uniform-lean blades with raking-light striations

LAYERS: (1) MATRIX WEDGES — 2 pale triangles (white alpha 0.04-0.06) at opposite corners: the white quartz matrix blades grow in. (2) BLADES — 9-14 (panel 4-6) long parallelograms ALL leaning the same diagonal, 68-75° from horizontal with per-blade jitter of only ±4° (uniform lean IS kyanite); widths 0.035-0.10w, spanning full height with overshoot; x-origins on a jittered 1D grid so spacing varies; let 2-3 blades slightly overlap neighbors. Keeps the current geometry idea (lines 269-283) but fewer, wider, and lit. (3) BLADE FILL — linearGradient ACROSS the blade width: near edge lerp(primary,Black,0.45) a0.10 -> center primary a0.14 -> far edge lerp(primary,White,0.25) a0.08, same lit side for every blade (shared light) — deep sapphire core fading to pale steel edge. (4) FEATHER ZONING — on every 3rd blade, 1-2 translucent quads crossing the blade at ~90° to its axis, lerp(primary,White,0.4) alpha 0.06-0.09: the alternating light/dark cross-bands from the references. (5) STRIATIONS — per blade 3-5 hairlines strictly parallel to the blade axis and clipped to its bounds, alternating white / lerp(primary,White,0.5), width 0.7-1px, alpha 0.08-0.15: the ripple lines that catch raking light. (6) LIT EDGE — 1dp secondary stroke, alpha 0.15-0.20, on the lit long edge of 3-4 foreground blades (pearly luster). Panel: no zoning quads, alphas x0.45. DO NOT DRAW: crisscrossing directions (crisscross = Rose Quartz cracks); pointed terminations (Clear Quartz); circles/dots of any kind; striations escaping blade bounds. BUDGET: 14 blades x (fill + ~5 lines + occasional quad/edge) ≈ 100 mostly-line ops; fully static/cacheable (the existing animated BACKDROP_SHARDS can stay as the only moving accents, or be retired).

*Source: Starborn/Mystic Minerals/Crystals.eu blade descriptions; feather zoning + raking-light striation notes from search snippets; current impl Crystal.kt lines 269-283*

### ONYX recipe — straight silver band-lines with layer-trapped flecks (no gold)

Research verdict on 'gold flecks?': metallic gold flecks belong to gold-sheen obsidian and gold/amber VEINS to architectural onyx marble — both real looks, but gold is Lapis's signature channel in this app, and the Onyx theme's secondary (0xFFA7B7D1 silver-steel) keeps the recipe cool automatically. Use secondary for all metallics; never hardcode gold. LAYERS: (1) DEPTH WASH — vertical linearGradient lerp(background,Black,0.5) -> transparent over the top 40%, alpha ~0.15: polished-black depth. (2) BANDS — 10-16 (panel 6-8) full-width near-straight band paths; vertical wobble amplitude only 0.5-1.5% of h over 2 cubic segments (the current 4% wobble at lines 284-298 is too wavy — onyx bands are explicitly straight/parallel vs agate's curves; straightness is Onyx's differentiator vs every other theme). Spacing: fract01-hashed 0.04-0.12h gaps arranged in 2-3 'families' of 3-5 close bands separated by wider empty gaps (agate rhythm). (3) BAND RENDERING — each band is a PAIR: bright core line lerp(secondary,White,0.3), width 1-1.5px, alpha 0.20-0.30, plus a soft parallel dark stroke just below it, near-Black, width d*0.008, alpha ~0.10 — light line over dark gap reads as a polished layer edge. Hierarchy: every 4th band in primary (moonlit blue) alpha ~0.15; every 6th pure white alpha ~0.25. (4) FLECKS — 18-30 micro dots 0.5-1.5dp in secondary / lerp(secondary,White,0.4), alpha 0.15-0.35, with ~60% clustered within a few px of 2 chosen band lines (inclusions trapped between layers); reuse the existing Onyx twinkle-star layer for these so the animation budget is unchanged — just re-bias positions toward bands and cap count. (5) CUT EYE (optional) — 3 nested OPEN half-ellipse arcs entering from one canvas edge, stroke secondary alpha ~0.10: hints at agate curvature without closing rings. Panel: no eye, flecks 6-8, alphas x0.4. DO NOT DRAW: gold/warm hues; closed ring systems (Malachite); wobble beyond 1.5%h. BUDGET: 16 bands x 2 strokes + 3 arcs ≈ 35 static ops + ≤30 animated fleck alphas.

*Source: Healing-sounds onyx-agate parallel-band note; Mindat agate-vs-onyx thread; GemSociety; The World of Stone black onyx slabs; Moonrise Crystals gold-sheen obsidian (for the flecks verdict); current impl Crystal.kt lines 284-298*


## References

### Reference — Malachite: banded concentric 'bullseye' structure

Defining structures from polished-slab photography and mineralogy captions: botryoidal (grape-dome) growth sliced flat yields CONCENTRIC RING SYSTEMS — 'bullseye' eyes of many nested rings around multiple nucleation centers, with bands from adjacent domes merging into shared contour-map lines between eyes. Bands alternate near-black forest green and bright emerald/mint (copper concentration varies per growth layer); the rhythm is characteristically UNEQUAL — thick dark bands separated by thin bright lines. Rings are never geometric circles: each has low-frequency wobble and slight ovality. Fibrous radial microstructure gives a silky, sometimes chatoyant sheen — a soft luminous streak that sweeps across the surface at an angle. Plume/ribbon passages fill regions between eye systems. Drawable essence: 2-3 wobbled-concentric ring systems + merging S-contours + one diagonal sheen streak, thick-dark/thin-bright stroke alternation.

### Reference — Clear Quartz: prisms, fog gradient, phantoms, iridescent fractures

Defining structures from cluster/macro photography descriptions: hexagonal PRISMS with straight parallel sides tapering slightly to sharp pyramidal TERMINATIONS, rising at varied tilts from a milky base. The body is glassy and near-colorless — the stone is read almost entirely through its EDGES (bright specular outlines, one lit face per light direction) and its FOG GRADIENT: translucent milky cloud at the base clearing to water-clear at the tip. Internal features: PHANTOMS (faint ghost outlines of earlier, smaller terminations nested inside a prism), thin internal fracture planes that flash subtle RAINBOW iridescence, and sparse point inclusions (hematite/sulphide specks) that read as tiny sparkles. Drawable essence: 4-6 edge-lit tapering shafts with 2-facet tips, along-axis white fade, 1-2 inset ghost tips, 1-2 short gradient-stroked crack polylines, sparse twinkles inside prism bounds. Colors stay near-neutral ice; saturation is the failure mode.

### Reference — Kyanite: bladed habit, lengthwise striations, feather zoning

Defining structures from specimen photography descriptions: long FLAT BLADE crystals (like frozen steel ruler shards) growing in sub-parallel sprays — all blades lean the same direction with only a few degrees of fan. Each blade carries FINE STRIATIONS running along its length that 'catch raking light like ripples on water'; luster is pearly on cleavage faces. Color zoning is strong: deep sapphire/indigo down the blade center fading to pale steel-blue or white at the edges, plus FEATHER ZONING — alternating lighter/darker translucent bands crossing the blade roughly perpendicular to its axis. Blades often sit in white quartz matrix visible at the margins. Drawable essence: 9-14 same-lean parallelograms, across-blade dark-core-to-pale-edge gradient with one shared lit side, 3-5 clipped axis-parallel hairlines per blade, occasional perpendicular pale cross-band, white matrix wedges at corners. The uniform lean direction is the identity — any crisscross destroys it.

### Reference — Onyx: straight parallel banding; gold flecks belong to other stones

Defining structures: true onyx is the banded chalcedony whose layers are STRAIGHT and PARALLEL — mineralogy sources explicitly contrast it with agate's curved, chaotic bands; bands are 'smooth and uniform', giving the sleek polished look. Classic palette: jet black body with crisp white/pale band lines, in grouped families of close bands separated by wide dark gaps. On the user's 'gold flecks?' question: (a) metallic gold FLECK shimmer is gold-sheen obsidian — thousands of microscopic golden flecks from trapped gas bubbles in black volcanic glass — a different stone; (b) gold/amber VEINING appears in architectural black 'onyx marble' (backlit slab trade: 'solid jet-black base... veining in gold, amber, or white'). Both are authentic looks, but in MusicViz gold is Lapis's pyrite signature, and the Onyx theme's anchors are cool (primary 0xFF6FA8FF, secondary 0xFFA7B7D1) — so the recipe renders flecks/bands in parametric secondary silver-steel, which keeps the stone-vs-stone separation and honors the app's derive-from-theme rule. Drawable essence: 10-16 near-ruler-straight band pairs (bright hairline over soft dark gap), band families, sparse micro-flecks clustered along band lines, optional open edge arcs.
